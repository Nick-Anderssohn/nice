“From theme” — a theme-driven entry in the Accent row

A sixth entry meaning derive the accent from the active theme instead of a fixed preset. It renders as a diagonally split disc: the upper-left half is the accent your light theme yields, the lower-right is your dark theme’s. Two halves say “this changes with context,” and no preset has two colors — so it can’t be misread as just another swatch. It sits apart behind a hairline divider, keeps the row’s exact selection grammar (1px ink ring, 2px offset), and — because the Accent row is scheme-independent — one disc honestly shows what you’ll get in both modes at once. Change the example themes below and watch the halves, the hint, and the window chrome retint live.

Anatomy

  • 16px disc split at 135°: upper-left = light theme’s derived accent, lower-right = dark theme’s. Both halves live.
  • 1px seam in the window surface color → reads as two chips, not a gradient; self-adapts per scheme.
  • Selection ring unchanged — 1px ink, 2px offset, footprint reserved. Exactly accent_control’s grammar, no collision.
  • 1px hairline divider sets it apart from the five fixed presets.
  • Hint line when selected names the source theme + shows the effective color; a11y label “From theme”.

Where the derived color comes from (landed 2026-08-02)

The accent tints more than the caret (selection wash, chrome, logo), so “From theme” needs a source color and a fallback. The landed chain: the theme’s own accent slot (TerminalTheme.accent — hand-curated for every built-in, file-declared for future Warp-style imports) → ansi[4] blue for imported themes that declare none. Flip either picker to “Imported · ansi 4 fallback” to see the fallback in action.

Cursor and foreground are deliberately NOT in the chain — they are near-monochrome by design (they must contrast with everything) and make washed-out chrome; Warp’s own Catppuccin file declares accent: #b4befe and points the cursor at it, not the reverse. An earlier revision of this mock recommended cursor→foreground; that is superseded. Under “From theme” the caret still honors theme.cursor when set — only the accent derivation excludes it. The hint line names the source theme.
